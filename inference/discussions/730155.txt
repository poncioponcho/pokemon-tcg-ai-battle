How do strong agents decide whether to search before drawing?

Hi everyone,

I am trying to understand how strong agents handle information order within a turn. A common choice is whether to use a deterministic search action first or draw first.

Searching first can thin the deck and avoid drawing a card that could have been searched, but drawing first can reveal what is missing and preserve the search action for a better target. The correct order also seems to depend on the current hand, known deck contents, once-per-turn actions, and whether the deck is search-heavy or draw-heavy.

If you are comfortable sharing broad design choices:

Do you encode this as hand-written ordering rules, learn it from full-turn sequences, or compare short action sequences with search/rollouts?
For behavior cloning, do you train each decision independently, or preserve the complete order of actions within the turn?
How do you treat two action orders that reach a similar board but expose different information along the way?
Have you found search-heavy decks easier or harder for learned policies than draw-heavy decks?  Broad method categories are enough; no code or deck-specific strategy is needed. Thanks!
